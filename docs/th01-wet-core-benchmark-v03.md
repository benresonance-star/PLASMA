# TH-01 | Wet Core v0.3 | Engineering benchmark

**Status: semantic-model research candidate. Not a compliant or construction-ready design.**

The reference is a compact two-level wet core (4.8 m × 3.6 m footprint, 3.0 m storey increment) with an upper basin, shower and WC; lower laundry and powder-room WC; two manifold assemblies; a heat-pump hot-water cylinder; meter, traps, fittings and a sanitary stack. A service chase, wall stud frame, joists and slab are included as physical objects.

## Distinguishing model principles
- Every installed fixture and piece of equipment is a parametric component with **typed connection ports**, not a centreline destination.
- Supply and sanitary drainage are separate *directed graphs*. A water heater has a cold inlet and a hot outlet. The stack has separate fixture branch ports and an outfall; venting is represented but **not designed or validated**.
- Physical routes have real millimetre centreline dimensions, materials and named fittings. Generic fitted components are not claims about any manufacturer's product.
- Model objects retain stable IDs across changes; UI geometry must remain a **derived representation**, consistent with ADR-001.
- Structural interactions are reported as unresolved review items and never hidden or auto-authorised.
- A basin position parameter can be changed without rebuilding unrelated fixture/component identities. Proposal and approval remain outside this package.

## Executable gates
\`pnpm --filter @spds/wet-core build\`
\`pnpm --filter @spds/wet-core typecheck\`
\`pnpm --filter @spds/wet-core test\`

Tests cover connectivity, stable IDs, moved-fixture dependency impact, intentionally failed drainage gradient, broken supply graph, structural interference, BOM metrics and unverified engineering status.

## Known limitations
- Pipe/fitting geometry is a generic installable-family approximation; **no actual manufacturer certification**.
- Sanitary slope benchmark 1:60 is a chosen geometric assertion, not an AS/NZS 3500 rule or compliance claim.
- Waste branches do not yet have manufacturer-matched swept-junction angles, real trap seals, hydraulic fixture units, vent calculations, supports, structural opening permissions or water-control-plane detailing.
- Piping routes are authored as a *semantic construction fixture* for rigorous testing, not generated by an autonomous hydraulic design solver. A selected route can still intersect a joist and is flagged accordingly.
- Further gates: standards-versioned rule adapters; valid fittings/couplers; slope- and framing-aware routing; CAD-derived collision geometry; agent ChangeSet proposal/approval; drawing/IFC schedule reconciliation.
