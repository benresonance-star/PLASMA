Plasma Terrain Studio

Toolset, editing contracts and execution pipeline · v0.2

Status: proposed implementation specification · red-team revision. This document supersedes v0.1 as the preferred terrain implementation direction. It incorporates the September 2026 red-team review while preserving v0.1 as lineage. The changes are architectural requirements, not claims that the prototype already implements them.

Design goal: an architect can shape land freely, enter exact levels, understand what controls the result, compare alternatives and carry a credible surface through grading, quantities and documentation. An AI can perform the same work through bounded, inspectable operations. Neither editing route can silently rewrite survey evidence, lose relationships or disguise incomplete checks.

The governing decisions are: semantic controls own design; raw evidence is immutable; generated geometry is replaceable; event history is not the active terrain definition; compiled terrain state may be canonically compacted without losing audit history; every result identifies its inputs, quality and currentness; dependency propagation is evaluator-specific; expensive checks refine asynchronously; commitment and release are separate decisions.

1. Product experience

The working surface opens with 3D, site plan and a movable section/profile. A compact tool rail contains Select, Draw, Shape, Grade, Inspect and Compare. The selected object's properties show its exact values, drivers, affected objects and outstanding checks. Existing, proposed and difference views are available without leaving the edit. Selection is shared across views.

The user should be able to perform these tasks without prompting an AI:

  • Draw a polygon, give it an RL or slope, and grade its edges back to existing ground.
  • Pick a contour, move it precisely, and choose whether to edit its controlling feature or create a new contour constraint.
  • Raise a selected patch 150 mm while holding its perimeter and preserving a ridge.
  • Draw a driveway from a surveyed crossover to a garage threshold; adjust its plan, profile and crossfall independently.
  • Move a pad and see the building, approach, cut/fill and unresolved constraints respond together.
  • Delete a grading feature, inspect the resulting surface and downstream consequences, then undo the whole action.
  • Ask an AI for three grading alternatives within a region and inspect the differences before choosing one.

A brush stroke, numeric edit and AI instruction all compile to the same command family. The ordinary user sees design consequences; developers can expand the diagnostic record.

Do not infer “existing”, “natural”, “surveyed” or a statutory ground definition from a filename. These are different roles. A project may have multiple dated existing-ground interpretations and separately declared regulatory baselines. Epistemic status and workflow status are independent. A human can accept an assumed level for design development; acceptance does not convert it into an observation.

3. Representation strategy

Use a constrained 2.5D triangulated surface for irregular site terrain: one elevation for each valid XY location in each named surface domain. Author points, boundaries and breaklines; triangulate their registered XY arrangement and attach elevations. Use raster tiles for source DEMs, brush fields, dense analysis and previews where appropriate. Do not resample every source to a permanent coarse grid.

Terrain is a representation of the ground system, not the ontology of the ground itself. Keep the type system open to topographic surfaces, material volumes, interfaces, excavations, structures and voids. Ordinary topography remains a 2.5D surface because that is the efficient authoritative representation for most site design; soil strata, groundwater, basements, trenches, tunnels, retaining structures and undercuts are separate world objects or volumes linked through explicit interfaces rather than forced into a height field.

Maintain separate render LODs. A coarse display mesh must not become the basis of final quantities or setting-out levels. Source density, analysis resolution, contour interval and screen tessellation are four separate controls.

A height surface cannot faithfully encode two elevations at the same XY. Represent a retaining step with adjoining upper/lower surface patches and a separate retaining-face object. Represent bridges, rock overhangs, tunnels and undercuts as explicit mesh/solid objects with documented interfaces to ground. Never simulate a vertical wall by an arbitrarily tiny horizontal offset and call it robust geometry.

Triangle identities are realization-local. Persistent selections use feature IDs, control IDs, station/offset anchors or surface coordinates with a defined remapping policy. A triangle deleted by remeshing cannot remain an authoritative attachment target.

4. Units, registration and numerical policy

Continue using millimetres in Plasma local design commands for compatibility. Geospatial adapters retain their native units and convert through a recorded transform. Use Float64 for local computation and a local origin near the site; convert to Float32 only for camera-relative rendering. Do not calculate geometry from rounded screen labels.

Every imported dataset declares horizontal CRS or named local frame; vertical datum or explicitly unknown datum; axis order; horizontal and vertical units; coordinate epoch when applicable; source date; coverage; transformation pipeline and required grid assets. Missing transformation grids or an unknown datum prevent a claim of registered agreement. Permit an explicitly labelled local assumption branch, preserving the unknown.

Never silently fit scale or vertical offset to make mismatched sources look aligned. Registration proposals show control points, residual vectors, transformation family, estimated uncertainty and excluded controls. A rigid fit and a scale-changing fit are different choices. Map imagery is context unless independently qualified for the intended measurement.

Keep distinct tolerances for snapping, point deduplication, topology predicates, boundary continuity, solver residual, interpolation error and survey uncertainty. A 1 mm snapping increment is not 1 mm survey accuracy. A numerical policy is versioned with each realization. Initial local coincidence tolerance may be 0.1 mm for normalized design inputs, but must be validated against the dataset and operation; source observations are not discarded merely for falling within it.

5. Object lifecycle and CRUD

“Delete” is a design operation with a dependency policy, not removal from an array. Prefer suppress/restore for experimentation. A committed deletion creates a tombstone and lineage record; physical blob collection follows retention rules only after references are released.

Event history is not the active terrain definition. The append-only history records how the world changed for audit, undo, provenance and replay. The live terrain compiler consumes the current semantic intent state, not an ever-growing replay stack of every historical gesture. A checkpoint plus event tail must reproduce committed intent and results within the numeric contract, but ordinary evaluation is free to use a compact canonical state.

Canonicalisation and feature compaction: compatible local edits may be fused into a stable semantic feature or compiled patch when this can be proved behaviorally equivalent for all declared queries and dependencies. For example, a series of accepted brush strokes may compile into one SculptRegion while retaining the original strokes in history. Compaction never erases provenance, changes ownership, hides conflicts or crosses hard semantic boundaries. The compiler records the source operation set, compaction algorithm/version and an equivalence digest. Compaction is rejected when equivalence cannot be demonstrated.

Locking a feature can freeze its parameters, its realized world placement, or both; these are separate explicit modes. A pad-driven building lock must report the indirect pad movement it blocks.

6. Selection and precise manipulation

Support object, control, edge, region and surface selection. Provide click/tap cycling, visibility isolation, box/lasso selection, named sets and filters by feature type or status. Multi-selection exposes shared parameters and mixed values; numeric changes declare absolute versus relative semantics.

Reference choices: world XY/Z; local feature axes; surface tangent/normal; alignment station/offset; relative to a named RL; relative to a sampled baseline. The active frame is always visible near numeric entry. Snap candidates include surveyed points, endpoints, intersections, breaklines, contours and alignment stations, with a ranked and cycleable result.

Desktop: click-select, drag handles, type distance/level, constrain axis, duplicate with explicit mode, undo. iPhone: navigation is the default; select an object then activate an edit handle. Use large hit targets around small controls, a magnified pick lens, finger-offset cursor and numeric bottom sheet. Separate Move, Level and Rotate affordances. Two-finger navigation must cancel or suspend a one-finger edit predictably; pointer cancellation rolls back the uncommitted gesture. Do not rely on hover, right-click, pressure or a keyboard modifier.

TerrainEditSession fast path: pointer and touch movement must not execute the full durable transaction pipeline on every event. Beginning an edit resolves the target, frame, local semantic/spatial scope, short lease, adjacency and preview kernel once. During the gesture, deltas update a transient local candidate with bounded work; superseded transient results are discarded. Gesture end compiles the interaction into one or more semantic operations and sends those through full impact planning, validation, revision checks, commit and propagation. Accepted world state is never mutated directly by the gesture loop.

  resolve target/frame/scope
  cache local topology + drivers
  acquire short lease
      ↓
GESTURE LOOP
  delta → cheap local preview → display

GESTURE END

While dragging, display starting value, delta and proposed value; snap source; influence boundary; locked controls; local conflicts. Keep the camera stable. Release finishes the gesture into a proposal. Preserve the last valid preview if an input becomes invalid, but clearly show the attempted invalid input; never silently clamp it and report success.

A selected derived contour offers: inspect level, edit controlling feature, or promote this segment to a design constraint. Moving a contour means altering the terrain definition; changing label placement is representation-only. Contour constraints are isoheight polylines and must be revalidated against crossing contours and other elevation constraints.

Brush invariance: resample pointer input by world-space path distance, then evaluate a recorded kernel against a pinned input revision. Effect must not depend on event frequency or frame rate. A stationary hold accumulates only in a deliberately selected time-based airbrush mode. Store resampled samples, units, mask revision, kernel version, amplitude, radius and ordering—not just the raw device events.

Raise/lower is a displacement feature. Flatten targets an explicit plane or level. Smooth reads a pinned input surface and respects breaklines, holes, locked points and selected boundaries; it must not progressively erase its own evidence through unordered in-place updates. A volume-preserving smoothing option requires a declared conservation domain, correction method and residual; never imply that ordinary smoothing preserves volume.

A “set exact level” operation on generated terrain must name what takes ownership: edit an existing controller or create a local grade feature. Free vertex edits to a realization are either temporary inspection experiments or promoted into durable controls before commitment.

8. Pads, driveways and grading interfaces

Pad: arbitrary simple polygon with holes where meaningful; level, sloped or stepped subregions; building attachment; floor-to-pad relationship; excavation allowance; graded transition per edge. A side can daylight to a named surface, retain, remain fixed or meet another feature. Distinguish finished floor, finished ground, formation/subgrade and excavation bottom; a single “pad RL” cannot own all four.

A building footprint-fit rule is a configurable design constraint. The current prototype's hard fit gate is a useful default, not a universal architectural truth: cantilevers, piles and split-level foundations need explicit alternate support intent and evaluators. Never relax a real external obligation by changing a design preference.

Driveway/corridor: stable horizontal alignment segments, parameterized chainage, editable vertical profile, cross-section family, width and crossfall functions, shoulders, kerbs and grading extents. State whether profile stationing uses plan length; use that consistently. Changing alignment either preserves station distances, normalized positions or named anchors according to explicit policy, and reports displaced or invalid stations.

Road tie, garage threshold and intermediate profiles are independently addressable anchors. The garage threshold may follow building floor level plus a declared offset; it should not always follow pad elevation. Connect a corridor to a building through a named entrance/garage anchor, retaining the source road tie as evidence.

At grade changes, create tangent/transition geometry and evaluate a specified vehicle envelope, including approach, departure and breakover where relevant. A maximum longitudinal grade check alone is not a vehicle access assessment. Crossfall transitions and drainage checks are separate. Radius, transition length and grade targets come from the project's chosen requirements and vehicle, not hidden defaults presented as code compliance.

Daylighting: intersect the proposed batter with its target surface along an adaptive search; report no-hit, multiple-hit and coverage-limit cases. A fixed blend radius is only a schematic option. Do not force a boundary intersection when the required batter extends beyond ownership, survey coverage or an exclusion zone.

Retaining: upper/lower ground interfaces, face/crest/toe lines, height and drainage assumptions. Generate schematic geometry and quantities separately from structural adequacy. Future structural or geotechnical services consume these interfaces without taking ownership of the terrain recipe.

9. Composition and overlapping features

Use a typed dependency graph with explicit spatial masks and boundary conditions. The UI may show a readable feature list, but arbitrary visual layer order must not silently decide engineering precedence.

For every point in a surface domain, the compiler can explain which baseline and feature determine its level. Supported composition modes initially are replace-within-region, add-displacement and explicitly defined blend-to-baseline. Minimum/maximum envelopes can be added as named cut/fill operations with documented side effects. Persist all modes and their dependencies.

Two independently authored hard level regions overlapping with incompatible levels produce a conflict. The user can trim, partition, add a transition, set explicit precedence, or change one intent. “Last operation wins” is not an acceptable hidden policy. A brush clipped by a pad must show its effective mask and explain why it has no visible influence there.

A blend samples a named upstream surface revision and owns only its declared transition domain. It cannot read its own final output recursively. Iterative coupled solvers are contained in named solve groups with residuals, iteration budgets and failure states; general graph cycles are rejected.

Composition and solving are different mechanisms. Use deterministic composition for ordered, explainable feature interaction where one operation can be evaluated from declared upstream surfaces. Use a first-class SolveGroup when several objectives or constraints must be satisfied simultaneously—for example, hold boundary levels, fall away from a building, meet a driveway threshold, avoid a tree zone, limit slope and minimise cut. A solve group declares variables, hard constraints, soft objectives, domains, weights or priorities, solver/version, tolerances, iteration/resource budgets and residuals. Its output is a named semantic result, not a hidden mutation of unrelated features.

A surface query returns {status, z, normal, ownerRefs, inputRevisions, uncertainty, quality}. Status distinguishes valid, outside-domain, no-data, ambiguous interface and pending. It never returns zero for unknown elevation. Querying a retaining edge requires a side or returns both candidates explicitly.

10. Interaction sessions and durable transaction pipeline

There are two execution paths with one authoritative commit boundary. Interaction sessions keep direct manipulation fluid by reusing resolved scope and local caches while producing transient candidates. Durable transactions provide correctness, concurrency, provenance and release semantics. The interaction path may approximate; the commit path may not silently inherit an approximation.

  1. Discover: return capabilities, schemas, object identities, current revisions, frames and permitted scope.
  2. Resolve: resolve selectors to stable IDs and reference revisions; pin the read set and spatial scope.
  3. Normalize: validate units, coordinate frames, numeric ranges, schema, authorization and resource budget.
  4. Plan impact: compute semantic dependents, spatial influence, affected boundaries, read/write sets and required checks.
  5. Preview: evaluate an isolated candidate at interaction fidelity; show differences and pending checks.
  6. Evaluate: run local geometry and invariant checks; refine terrain, volumes and domain assessments asynchronously.
  7. Review: show conflicts, effects, evidence and alternatives; allow compatible subsets only after re-evaluation.
  8. Commit: compare relevant revisions and atomically append the operation receipt, world mutation and derived-result references.
  9. Propagate: schedule unresolved regional/global work with explicit status; do not relabel old results as current.
  10. Release: create a reproducible output package only when checks required for that declared purpose pass or have allowed, recorded dispositions.

Distinguish transient gesture preview, durable proposal, accepted design-development revision and released engineering/documentation package. Geometric invalidity and unresolved hard constraints block acceptance. Explicitly soft design conflicts can be accepted with disposition. Missing analysis can remain pending in a design-development revision, but blocks any release requiring that analysis. The release manifest states the scope actually checked.

A worker result is installable only if its object revisions, dependency digest, algorithm version, tolerance policy and target branch still match. Superseded results may enter a content-addressed cache; they cannot overwrite the visible current candidate.

Every committed mutation emits a change receipt. At minimum it records normalized operations, changed semantic facts, direct dependents, affected spatial domains, evaluator invalidations, recomputed outputs, pending/stale outputs, performance counters, authority/currentness transitions and the resulting world revision. The ordinary UI can compress this to a sentence; diagnostics and agents can inspect the full receipt. Receipts are the bridge between explainability, runtime profiling, debugging, provenance and future learning.

Undo is a new compensating transaction on the relevant history, restoring feature state and ownership. It must not simply restore an old render mesh. Concurrent work requires conflict-aware undo; unrelated later edits remain intact. A checkpoint plus event tail must reproduce commitments, not just visual similarity.

11. Human and AI contract

Natural language is an optional front end. The authoritative interface is a versioned command registry with JSON schemas, units by field, capabilities, preconditions, effect bounds, validation policy and structured error codes. No arbitrary executable script or untyped mesh replacement is part of the ordinary editing API.

Human/AI parity applies at the authoritative mutation boundary, not necessarily at the reasoning abstraction. Provide a higher-level intent/proposal layer above the terrain command registry. An AI or optimiser may reason in goals such as “reduce excavation while preserving the garage threshold and eastern boundary”, produce alternatives and then compile the selected proposal into the same bounded typed terrain commands used by human actions. The lower command API remains the only ordinary route to accepted state.

The server resolves the named scope, verifies permissions and computes the actual effect; the caller cannot assert a fake impact envelope. Separate read, preview, propose, commit and release capabilities. Current prototype AI remains proposal-only. Later bounded auto-commit is an explicit user-granted capability tied to object scope, operation types, budgets and expiry; AI cannot grant itself authority or silently soften constraints.

Reads support queryRegion, sampleSurface, section, explainAt, getDependencies, getChanges and getJobStatus. Large reads are paginated or tiled and identify completeness. AI output is a candidate set of semantic operations, with assumptions, metrics and affected features—not thousands of screen coordinates.

Request IDs are durable and idempotent. Reusing an ID with changed normalized content is an error. The same normalized command with the same pinned inputs must reproduce the same authoritative result within the declared numeric contract.

Object revision guards improve concurrency beyond a whole-world counter: disjoint edits may rebase only after proving disjoint semantic dependencies and influence domains, including shared boundaries. Overlapping brushes, competing level setters and linked pad/threshold edits do not auto-merge merely because their direct target IDs differ. Spatial queries also retain index/range revision guards so a newly inserted intersecting feature cannot escape conflict detection.

Human gestures hold short edit leases on their affected scope. AI proposals can continue elsewhere; overlapping work waits or branches. Leases coordinate interaction but never replace optimistic validation. On reconnect, offline operations remain proposals until checked against current state.

Structured failures include FRAME_MISMATCH, UNKNOWN_DATUM, SOURCE_CONFLICT, LOCKED_DEPENDENCY, OUTSIDE_COVERAGE, OVERLAPPING_WRITERS, INVALID_TOPOLOGY, STALE_READ, SOLVER_INFEASIBLE and RESOURCE_LIMIT. Each response supplies affected IDs, spatial location, evidence and possible repair actions.

12. Geometry robustness pipeline

Ingestion validates format, counts, finite coordinates, coverage, units and frame metadata before any geometry operation. Enforce size limits and bounded parsing; a malformed source cannot exhaust the interactive process. Preserve file digests and importer versions.

Normalize design constraints into a planar arrangement before triangulation. Split XY crossings, preserve parent-to-subsegment lineage and compare their Z conditions. Crossing breaklines with inconsistent elevations are a semantic conflict or separate physical layers; an exact intersection algorithm cannot resolve that design ambiguity.

Handle duplicate XY observations through explicit source reconciliation. Agreement within uncertainty can support a merged interpretation; disagreement stays visible. Collinear and cocircular inputs, repeated polygon vertices, tiny edges, near-intersections and large coordinates must have deterministic, tested handling. Holes remain holes. Do not triangulate across a no-data boundary merely because the convex hull contains it.

Use robust predicates and suitable exact constructions where intersections require them. “Exact” here describes arithmetic/topology behavior, not truth of the survey. Constraint moves must remove/reinsert or rebuild the affected constraint arrangement safely; do not assume a generic triangulation vertex-move method preserves constrained edges. CGAL's documentation explicitly distinguishes intersection modes and notes limits on constrained vertex movement. CGAL triangulations

A local topology change can propagate beyond a fixed tile. Grow the triangulation cavity until boundary conditions are satisfied; if that cannot be bounded, schedule a broader rebuild and retain the interactive preview. Partition authoritative topology with shared boundary ownership. Render tiling does not imply that independent tile triangulations will agree.

Each published realization passes orientation, domain, no-crossing, edge incidence, constrained-edge preservation, finite-elevation and seam-consistency checks. Repair is a proposal with a before/after diff. Reject unrepaired invalid topology rather than smoothing away the evidence.

Persistent anchor grammar: stable object IDs are insufficient for derived geometric locations that may move or disappear after remeshing. Attachments and selections use typed anchors such as FeatureAnchor, ControlAnchor, EdgeParameterAnchor, ChainageOffsetAnchor, SurfaceXYAnchor, IntersectionAnchor, ExtremumAnchor and DerivedFeatureAnchor. Re-evaluation returns EXACT, REPROJECTED, AMBIGUOUS or LOST with displacement, candidate matches, confidence where meaningful and the remapping reason. Ambiguous or lost anchors cannot silently jump to a convenient new location.

Index features by spatial extent and maintain a semantic dependency graph plus spatial reverse dependencies. Spatial influence includes old and new locations, brush support, blend reach and solver-specific reach. Content-addressed patch and boundary summaries avoid scanning all project geometry on an ordinary edit. A changed boundary expands propagation; an unchanged, sufficiently conservative summary can stop it.

No universal boundary-changed flag. A spatial boundary can be unchanged for triangulation while an internal saddle change reroutes hydrology or alters earthworks. Propagation stopping is therefore evaluator-specific. Each analysis or realization family may publish a conservative InfluenceCertificate containing evaluator/version, input revisions, spatial domain, semantic inputs, boundary signature, internal summary, assumptions and the conditions under which propagation may stop. A certificate is invalidated whenever those conditions are no longer proven. False negatives—failing to propagate a real consequence—are treated as correctness failures.

Hydrology can have long-range effects. A small saddle change can reroute an entire catchment, so downstream connectivity invalidation must cross tile boundaries. The same principle applies to a relocated road tie or a shared baseline. Do not impose an arbitrary radius and declare global correctness.

Schedule by priority: input feedback, active geometry, required acceptance checks, visible detail, regional analyses, then background options. Use cancellation tokens, generation numbers, backpressure and bounded queues. Replace superseded transient jobs; persist meaningful user operations. Workers compute on immutable snapshots and transfer packed buffers; they do not mutate shared accepted state.

Currentness belongs to claims and outputs, not just the world. Terrain geometry, driveway profile, vehicle clearance, cut/fill, drainage, retaining checks and registration may simultaneously have different states such as CURRENT, CHECKED, PENDING, STALE, INDETERMINATE or NOT_EVALUATED. Representation resolvers must read that status before presenting derived graphics. A previously green compliance overlay cannot remain visually authoritative after its inputs have changed.

Start with a small adaptive CPU worker pool and instrument transfer overhead. GPU compute is a later acceleration for dense independent fields, brush previews and selected raster operations. Keep topology/commit validation in the authoritative CPU kernel initially. GPU or low-precision preview differences must never silently alter accepted geometry. Capability detection and a CPU fallback are mandatory; an iPhone must not require desktop GPU features.

Proposed performance gates, not measured achievements: at the declared iPhone test fixture, target p95 input-to-visible-preview below 50 ms and at least 30 fps during ordinary manipulation. Report worker time, serialization, main-thread time and input-to-photon proxy separately. Target local post-gesture checks below 250 ms at the small fixture; larger jobs show progress and remain cancellable. Validate thresholds on an actual device before claiming them.

Benchmark three fixed fixtures: the current small site; a 100,000-control regional context with a small edit; and a million-point imported source processed through preprocessing/streaming. Record source size, active mesh size, device, browser, memory peak, thermal duration, cold/warm cache and percentiles. Large-source ingestion success is not evidence that every point can be edited interactively on a phone.

14. Analysis, quantities and uncertainty

Earthworks: compare named existing and proposed surfaces over an explicit common domain and phase. For piecewise-linear TINs, overlay their planar subdivisions so both elevations are affine on each cell; split cells along zero elevation difference, then integrate signed difference. Report cut and fill separately. Without the zero-crossing split, net volume can conceal both. This is exact integration of the chosen piecewise-linear models subject to numerical handling, not exact knowledge of ground.

For raster/sampled previews, show resolution, excluded/no-data area and convergence evidence from refinement. Never present uncovered ground as zero volume. Topsoil stripping, unsuitable material, bulking, compaction and reusable material classes are separate assumptions and quantities. Do not sum overlapping pad, drive and landscape volumes if they already belong to one composed proposed surface.

Slope and access: evaluate along alignment and across sections at sufficient adaptive sampling, including extrema or analytic segment checks where possible. Record vehicle and criterion versions. A green slope overlay is not a general approval.

Water: distinguish geometry-only flow direction/catchment screening from rainfall-runoff modelling and hydraulic simulation. Pits can be real; depression filling is an analysis transformation with retained provenance, not an automatic edit to existing ground. Flow assumptions, boundary conditions, inlets and outlets remain explicit.

Uncertainty: attach observation uncertainty, registration residuals, spatial correlation assumptions and interpolation uncertainty separately. A shared datum error is correlated across points; treating it as independent noise is misleading. Unknown uncertainty remains unknown. Evaluate robust pass, fragile pass, indeterminate and fail against the declared uncertainty model and acceptance criterion.

Rank additional information by decision sensitivity and acquisition cost: for example, a crossover spot level may matter more than dense points far from the proposed driveway. Use interval/sensitivity analysis initially. Statistical confidence or monetary value-of-information requires defensible distributions and consequence models; otherwise label the ranking as heuristic.

16. Persistence, interchange and output

A project package includes a versioned manifest, source references/digests, registration revisions, observation/interpretation recipes, semantic features, constraint policies, committed event history or checkpoints, branch references and evaluation manifests. Derived meshes may be included for quick opening but are checked against their input digests. Missing source blobs produce a recoverable degraded state, not silent reconstruction from a screenshot.

Persistence may include canonical compiled terrain state for fast opening, but it is always linked to the semantic intent revision and compaction/equivalence evidence. The event log remains the audit trail; the canonical state remains the efficient active definition; derived render meshes remain disposable caches.

Store stable feature IDs across edits. Split/merge operations record parent/child lineage. Imported source revisions retain correspondence maps where defensible; uncertain matches require review. Redo after a source update replays only with explicit rebase policy.

Prioritize CSV/XYZ points plus boundary/breakline JSON for the first interchange slice. Add GeoTIFF, LAS/LAZ and supplied civil surface formats through adapters with declared support matrices. A DXF line with Z is not automatically a surveyed contour; importing drawings requires layer/type interpretation. Do not claim full LandXML or IFC round-trip until supported entity subsets and independent re-import fixtures pass.

Exports name the surface role, revision, frame, units, datum, coverage, fidelity, algorithm versions, unassessed checks and applicable uncertainty. Drawings derive from view contracts: plan contours, spot levels, cut/fill plans, longitudinal profiles and sections can differ graphically while referring to the same world revision. Representation overrides cannot change terrain elevations.

19. Red-team decisions

A general “terrain mesh editor” is insufficient. It provides freedom but loses why a level exists. Preserve feature ownership and allow controlled escape routes through durable local override features.

A universal parametric graph is also insufficient. Enormous fine-grained graphs can make editing slow. Use semantic dependencies between features, spatial partitions within realizations and compact conservative boundary summaries.

More threads do not repair an unbounded dirty region. Measure invalidation and transfer cost first. Do not promise constant-time terrain editing; Delaunay changes, shared constraints and catchments can require broader work.

Everything cannot be automatically smooth. Ridges, kerbs and retaining discontinuities may be intentional. Smoothing has a named domain, protected features and explicit boundary conditions.

Flexibility does not mean silent policy changes. Let users alter design targets, composition and support assumptions explicitly. Preserve external obligations and observations with their authority intact.

An AI needs semantic handles more than screen control. Give it queries, stable IDs, exact commands, scoped alternatives and useful failures. Keep direct manipulation fast enough that the architect does not need AI for every millimetre.

A checked surface is not a checked site design.

History is evidence, not an execution plan. Preserve every accepted operation for audit and undo, but compile current intent into canonical state so long projects do not accumulate procedural entropy.

Direct manipulation has a privileged latency path. Gesture sessions may use bounded transient approximations, but all accepted changes cross the same durable validation and commit boundary.

Locality is evaluator-dependent. Geometry, hydrology, quantities, access and representation do not share one safe stopping rule. Use evaluator-specific conservative influence certificates and differential tests.

Currentness must be visible in representation. A visually polished but stale analysis is a correctness hazard. Graphics must reflect claim status and revision, not merely cached values.

Stable object identity does not solve derived attachment identity. Use typed persistent anchors and explicit remapping outcomes.

Do not force simultaneous design constraints into layer order. Deterministic composition and coupled solve groups are separate first-class mechanisms.

AI should reason above the command layer. Shared commands guarantee authority and auditability; a higher intent/proposal layer preserves AI leverage without granting uncontrolled world mutation.

T1R is a proof gate, not a feature milestone. The terrain program must demonstrate equivalence, bounded invalidation, long-history behavior, concurrency safety and real-device responsiveness before broadening the tool catalogue.

Geometry, survey quality, vehicle access, drainage, geotechnics, structure and statutory interpretation have separate evaluators and readiness states.

20. Starting point and decision record

The current prototype provides a synthetic 24 × 36 m ground grid, local edits, pad and two-segment driveway, worker terrain evaluation, approximate sampled volumes, revision-safe proposals and undo. The latest completed milestone places the existing townhouse on the pad in plan/3D, with offsets, floor offset, rotation, placement lock and footprint-fit conflict. Its all-16-test-file run passed; device/browser testing was not performed. It is still a schematic prototype.

This proposal replaces fixed-grid-only authoring with feature-owned constrained surfaces; replaces blanket pad priority with declared composition; distinguishes pad/formation/floor and threshold anchors; introduces proper lifecycle/deletion semantics; and turns pending analyses into explicit state. It preserves the existing transaction foundation and all five performance boundaries.

This companion is a design specification, not an update to implementation evidence or a claim of completing PLS-01/07/09/10/12. No new terrain tools were deployed as part of writing it. The previously completed building-placement milestone was successfully published to the existing Plasma site before this design request.
